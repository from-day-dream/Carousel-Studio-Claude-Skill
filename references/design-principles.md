# Design principles

Art direction layered on top of the retention framework. The brand's `design-language.md` always wins on specifics. These principles decide everything it doesn't cover.

## Negative space is the design
- Leave at least **a third** of every slide empty. Empty space is what makes the headline dominant and the slide scannable in 2 seconds.
- "Fits inside the margins" is not the bar. If a slide feels full, cut copy before shrinking type.
- Group related elements tightly and separate groups generously (e.g. 48px inside a group, 72–120px between groups). Uniform spacing everywhere reads as a template.

## Scale contrast
- The headline should be **2–3×** the key line's size. Small contrast looks timid; everything mid-size looks like a document.
- One big thing per slide. If a visual is big, the headline gets smaller or moves, never both big.
- Sizes come from the type scale tokens. Don't invent in-between sizes slide by slide.

## Rhythm across the carousel
- Alternate compositions: e.g. type stack → panel → inverse list → visual-led → centred CTA. Never all-identical compositions. On long carousels (8+) a repeating cycle of 3–4 compositions is fine and helps the series read as one.
- One inverse (dark) or otherwise "loud" slide per carousel is usually enough; it marks the turning point or the densest idea.
- Keep the *system* fixed (margins, counter, handle, markers, type families) while the *composition* varies. Variation without a system looks random; a system without variation looks templated.
- Check rhythm on the contact sheet: squint at the thumbnails. You should see a pattern, not a repeat.

## Image / type balance (when a slide has a visual)
- The visual carries the idea or it goes. Decoration competes with the headline.
- Either the image leads (big, text small and placed in its empty area) or the type leads (image small, framed, secondary). Never 50/50.
- Text over a busy area always gets a scrim or panel. The render report measures real pixel contrast.

## Copy is design
- Headlines are statements or promises, not labels. "Checking messages first" beats "Messages".
- Front-load the important word. Cut "really", "very", "just", "in order to", "the fact that".
- Each slide's copy must not repeat another slide's. The CTA slide doesn't summarise.
- Sentence case unless the brand says otherwise. No trailing periods on headlines.

## What "generic" looks like (avoid)
- Everything centred, every slide.
- Rounded cards with drop shadows holding each piece of text.
- Emoji or icons as bullet decoration on every line.
- Gradients the brand didn't ask for.
- Every word bold, or several accent colours fighting.
- Headline, body and list all within ~1.5× of each other in size.
- Every slide with the same composition and only the words changed.
- Stock-photo backgrounds with text slapped on top and no scrim.

## When output still looks templated
Make it more specific, not more decorated: tighten the headline, enlarge it, move the text block off-centre to a deliberate anchor, crop a decorative element off the edge, or switch one slide to the inverse composition.
